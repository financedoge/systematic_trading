Training a reviewer for actual trading decisions

28 October 2026 · Full ETF pool · Three main comparisons

Research on repeatedly inspected development periods, through 16 October 2026. Reviewer selection uses training data only, but this is not a fresh prospective test. Daily OHLC stop executions are modeled. No overlay, promotion or live changes.

  • A — standing protection: the unchanged reference strategy, with gap-aware stops and original trend exits.
  • B — trained price reviewer: separate per-ETF models estimate the benefit and downside of continuing a position or buying back after a sale. Only price and trade-context information enter the model.
  • C — trained price/volume reviewer: the same procedure, with three additional volume features. Both B and C may retain A when training fails to support a change. Online learning runs only as a diagnostic and cannot change trades.

__FORECASTS__

Loss is mean squared normalized return error plus 0.25 times squared normalized downside error, summarized by the median across ETFs with observations. Lower is better. “Constant” always forecasts the task's training mean. “Fitted” is the frozen traded model; “shadow” updates only after labels mature and never influences the trading account. Comparisons use exactly the same recorded decision contexts within each ETF/model/task. Different policies generate different contexts, so cross-policy forecast comparisons are descriptive. Overlapping labels do not provide independent observations.


Mathematical definition
__EQUATIONS__

O/H/L/C are dividend/split-adjusted prices; V is audited raw share volume. ATR is the Wilder 20-session average of true range. Flat daily bars use close location 0.5. The original HMM and stop multipliers are inherited unchanged. EMA features describe recent price behavior; they do not impose a new fixed trend regime or a fixed holding period.


Here r is the one-session log return. Ref is the entry fill while held and the last exit fill while waiting for recovery; Age counts sessions from that event. Peak is the highest closing price since entry, including the entry fill. For a hypothetical recovery before execution, Peak is max(last exit fill, current close), and its proposed stop starts at current close minus the inherited risk distance. Owned is one while a recovery is managed by the reviewer. Sigma is the inherited training volatility unit; d is the selected wide multiplier in aligned upward context and the tight multiplier otherwise. The entry risk unit remains fixed while the stop ratchets. f and s denote per-side fees and additional stop slippage. The regression intercept is unstandardized; I* penalizes the other coefficients, with only a 10−8 numerical ridge on the intercept.


Training examples correspond to decisions. Held examples come from closes where the reference strategy still holds and has no scheduled exit. Recovery examples come from flat closes 1–20 sessions after a sale, ending when the reference strategy buys again. Training also includes held states of hypothetical recoveries so the holding reviewer sees those states. Within each task, all rows from one actual holding episode, or from one originating sale and its overlapping hypothetical paths, share total weight one. This reduces domination by long trades without making observations statistically independent.


The label follows protection. From the next open, follow the existing stop, daily close ratchet and subsequent HMM/thesis exits for at most ten bars, with a terminal mark at the following open. A recovery follows reviewer ownership until an upward HMM confirmation; the label assumes no additional learned-review exit inside its path. R is gross incremental return, and D is adverse movement before the modeled exit. When a stop fills, later lows in that bar do not enter D. All labels are withheld until t+11, even if their path stopped sooner. A cancelled recovery has zero return/downside. Actual trades have no ten-day time exit; this is a forecast horizon.


Each ETF is fitted separately. Training uses the final 756 reference returns before the era cutoff. The final 252 sessions calibrate the reviewer; all prefix labels must mature before that boundary. Scalers and candidate reviewer coefficients use the prefix only. Regression chooses an intercept-only forecast or ridge strength 1, 10 or 100 by weighted validation error. Full regressions require at least eight source groups. The action margin is then selected from disabled, 0, 0.25 or 0.5 using a matched validation strategy replay and the stated return/drawdown guards. The selected regression is refitted on all matured training rows. No evaluation data selects coefficients, margin or activation.


The parent market filters, HMM and trading policy are the previously fitted outer-training snapshot throughout this reviewer calibration. Consequently, the internal validation is conditional on that snapshot; it is not fully nested validation of the complete parent-selection pipeline. The main evaluation only uses models fitted before its start, but these historical eras have been reviewed repeatedly. Neither training selection nor this study establishes future profitability.


One value rule replaces the confirmation stack. Buy back when estimated net recovery value exceeds the chosen margin; add an exit when continuing value falls below the negative margin. There is no separate bullish candle, volume-sign, accumulated evidence or HMM-entry requirement. The model may consider exit-price distance through its features, but there is no hard exit-price band. The next executable open must remain within one signal-date ATR of the signal close; otherwise the recovery order is cancelled. An ordinary qualifying entry takes priority over recovery when both agree to buy.


Recovery ownership delays HMM exit authority. Protection remains active from the opening fill. The learned holding reviewer manages a recovery until the original HMM confirms eligibility for an upward entry; then the original HMM/thesis exits also resume. The learned exit remains active throughout, so this handoff does not prevent the two learned models from contradicting each other. WEAT demonstrates that failure. A value-based exit can coincide with an original exit; its recorded reason alone does not establish an incremental trading change. Component P&L comparisons measure the combined effect. All original entries retain their original exits and may receive the new additional exit. No stop is widened, and no loss warning switches the traded model in this round.


Learning is measured in shadow. Separate linear sufficient statistics start around the final fitted coefficients with weight 20, forget with a 63-session half-life, and add each completed label at weight 1/10. Predictions are stored before their labels mature. The actual account always uses frozen coefficients. Predictive error on these decision samples is a diagnostic, not a proof of policy value or a calibrated posterior.


Execution, data and verification limits


Signals use completed closes and fill at the next open, or one further session in delay stress. A standing stop uses the level known before that day's low: if touched, fill at min(open, stop) less modeled slippage. Opening scheduled exits precede the stop test; protective fills cancel stale pending exits. Entry-day stops are allowed. Daily bars cannot establish spread, liquidity or the path through intraday jumps. No same-close fill is claimed.

Auditing does not prove historical publication vintages. Positions use fractional shares, fees on each side and terminal marking without forced sale. Raw HMM IC is unchanged across the three arms; forecast diagnostics concern the distinct protected-path targets.


Independent accounting reconstructs every ordered fill. Native Windows LEAN fixtures put frozen fills and marks on a synthetic event calendar to validate ordering, sizing, fees and NAV. They do not validate market execution or independently fit the reviewer. Interactive chart behavior is checked offline for every ETF/era/model; representative scientific figures are visually inspected. Browser layout is not certified.
