What this run tells us

The trend estimates are more useful, but the trading rule is not a general improvement yet. The fast-HMM models B and C have the stronger median Sharpe in 2023–October 2026; the simpler A does better across the pool in 2020–2022. These are the three predeclared comparisons, with no evaluation-driven winner hidden behind another variant name.

TLT improves from −18.53% under the earlier multiscale strategy to −11.45% with B and −12.48% with C, after 5 bp per side. Its full-period result is still negative. C improves EWA from −18.00% to +23.57% and MCHI from +7.95% to +52.95%, but CORN deteriorates from −9.86% to −14.19%. GLD returns +76.60% with C versus +85.78% previously; its actual training choice is A, returning +58.32%.

The dynamic overlay returns 13.46% CAGR / 1.372 Sharpe / 7.48% drawdown, versus FR25's 16.51% / 1.450 / 8.65%. The drawdown reduction does not compensate for the lost return under the declared acceptance rules. No asset-family gate passes; four earlier-period portfolio/friction gates pass, with none in the later period. This remains research. FR25 is an empirical benchmark whose regime dependence also needs testing; these frozen gates describe this experiment, not a universal requirement that every diagnostic model beat FR25.

Learning the timescale remains unresolved. All 102 slow fits reach the maximum allowed noise ratio, λL = 10−6; 100 of 102 fast fits reach λF = 10−2. The observation variances are fitted, but the timescale boundaries bind. A continuous slope is not proof of an unconstrained learned horizon. Both evaluation periods are already inspected development history.

The mathematics behind the three choices

1. Estimate a slow and a fast slope from price

Ct is the adjusted closing price, t is a trading session, j=L denotes slow and j=F fast. Each filter estimates a latent log-price level ℓ and a slope b in log-return per session. R is observation-noise variance and λR is slope-innovation variance. The two filters observe the same price separately; their levels are not added together.

__EQ_observation__
__EQ_state__

There is no fixed 21-, 63- or 252-session trend window. All preceding observations enter the forward filter, with weights determined by its covariance recursion. However, λ is restricted to separate slow and fast ranges and held fixed during evaluation. Gains converge after initialization, so the model does not keep changing its effective horizon with market conditions. Its fitted bounds are shown explicitly below.

2. Convert slope uncertainty into direction probabilities

σ̂r is the training standard deviation of daily log returns; v is the filtered slope variance. Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution. A small symmetric band δ defines the range state. Up, range and down probabilities sum to one.

__EQ_slope_law__
__EQ_direction__
__EQ_interval__

A uses these fast-slope probabilities directly. The same Gaussian slow probabilities provide context in all models. Slow-up/down is confirmed at probability ≥70%; an unconfirmed state means uncertainty, not automatically a range. A negative b̂L,t with positive Δb̂L,t describes flattening, but the present entry rule does not use that change separately.

3. B and C add a three-state HMM to the fast slope

z is the standardized filtered fast slope. S is the hidden down/range/up state; A is its transition matrix, μ the state mean and ν the shared variance. π is the forward state probability after the current observation. φ denotes the normal density.

__EQ_hmm__
__EQ_forward__

B and C substitute πt,+ and πt,− for the fast direction probabilities in the trading rules. This is a two-stage filter and classifier, not joint switching-Kalman inference. Filtered slope observations remain serially correlated, so the HMM probabilities must not be interpreted as calibrated confidence in a profitable trade.

4. Buy an upswing; hold through range; exit on down evidence or protection

dt is the desired long/cash state after close t; ht is the position actually held after the day's opening fill. Entry requires being flat, having no pending long target and being armed. A slow downtrend never vetoes a short upswing. After an exit signal, fast-up probability must fall below 50% to rearm.

__EQ_buy__

__EQ_context__

Lt is the protective level already in force at close t. A range state alone does not force a sale. A completed-close protection breach is an additional exit condition:

__EQ_sell__

Oe is the actual entry opening price; ts the entry signal date. M is the highest completed close since entry, with the entry open included. The old level is tested first; only afterward is tomorrow's level tightened. An adopted level never moves downward, even when slow context improves.

__EQ_trail__

Protection is measured in entry price × training volatility, not current ATR. It is tested at the close and executed at the following open, or one session later in the delay test. It is not an intraday stop or a guaranteed loss limit. Current volatility/volume tightening is not part of this isolation experiment. None of A/B/C uses volume as a signal.

5. Execution and net return

Δ is the extra execution delay; f is the fee per side. Bt=1 when h changes from 0 to 1, and Et=1 when it changes from 1 to 0. N is portfolio value; O is the adjusted opening price. Orders follow the frozen FIFO timing, including pending decisions.

__EQ_execution__
__EQ_accounting__

Idle cash earns zero in the individual-ETF test. A purchase reserves its fee, hence the factor 1/(1+f). Evaluation preserves a position already held at the boundary from the preceding simulation; it is not a fresh-funded deployment on that date.

How the three models compare across the pool

Pool summaries exclude BIL. “Within guards” means CAGR no more than 1 percentage point below the earlier multiscale benchmark and drawdown no more than 2 points worse. A family must improve median Sharpe and lower-quartile CAGR, while ≥90% of adequate fits satisfy both guards, across both eras and all frictions. Better median results alone do not pass.

What the previous names actually meant

The previous experiment used two discrete three-state chains, L (slow) and S (fast), rather than the continuous slopes above. The names combined three independent design choices. Here is their exact meaning; they are not extra choices in this report.

“Price” versus “volume”: what observations enter the model?

The price version reads daily log returns. The volume version adds the daily change in log audited raw volume V. Each coordinate is divided by its training standard deviation and clipped to ±5:

__EQ_old_observation__

The return-coordinate mean of the fitted Gaussian observation model is the sum of slow and fast state contributions, in standardized, clipped units:

__EQ_old_drift__

The volume coordinate has a separately fitted mean mij,v for each of the nine joint states, without an additive restriction or a zero range anchor. Covariance is diagonal and shared across states:

__EQ_old_emission__

Training standard deviations have a floor of 10−6; return-component magnitudes are constrained to [0.015,1.5]. Slow-state stay probabilities were restricted to 0.98–0.999; fast stays to 0.65–0.97.

“Uniform” versus “context”: when is the exit more tolerant?

Uniform used θt=θ0 and kt=k0 regardless of the slow state. Context raised the down-probability exit threshold to 65% and doubled the trailing distance when slow-up probability reached 55%:

__EQ_old_context__

The previous policy grid used entry thresholds {0.35,0.40,0.50}, exit thresholds θ0∈{0.35,0.50}, and k0∈{2,3,4}. Entry also required positive model-predicted next-session return; rearming required fast-up probability below 35%. Trail accounting and next-open execution used the same rules described above.

“Coupled context volume” therefore meant the coupled transition equation, the two-coordinate price-and-volume observation, and the 55%-triggered exit override. “Independent uniform price” meant the factorized transition equation, return-only observations, and unchanged exits. In TLT the previous slow probabilities never reached their context threshold, so the extra context rule never activated. That is why this experiment measures a continuous price slope directly.
