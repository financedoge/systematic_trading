The algorithm we will carry forward

Two forward Kalman filters estimate fast and slow price slopes. One three-state HMM classifies the fast slope using symmetric up/down distributions. Buy an eligible upward state after rearming; hold through neutral states; sell on sufficient downward evidence, persistent thesis failure where its training rule is enabled, or a closing protection breach. Slow upward context can make exits more patient. Parameters remain fixed within each tested era while the filtered states update daily.

Call this frozen building block symmetric-trend-sleeve-v1. It is an alias of entry-value-20261027-v1 / hmm, reproduced as stop-review / baseline. The 612 matched fee/delay results agree. It excludes the online return gate, Bayesian policy switching, loss-triggered resets, learned holding/recovery reviewer and standing intraday stop.

The choice respects your earlier preference for B and fixes the unequal-variance HMM's misleading directional tails. Its modest complexity makes the next portfolio experiment easier to interpret. It does not win every historical comparison: the older fixed detector has higher later-period median return and Sharpe. We retain that control and the standing-stop alternative in the evidence rather than relabeling either as part of the chosen algorithm.

Daily decision flow

Priority matters: execute the old scheduled target first; compute today's close evidence; rearm; test an existing desired long's exit; otherwise test a flat entry; then raise the next protection boundary. A close breach is not filled at that close. Pending targets remain on the delayed target stream. A new opposing signal can therefore follow an earlier scheduled order, especially in the extra-delay stress case.

Training lineage and thesis-rule selection

The chosen policy is deliberately a frozen composition of earlier research. First, separately fit each ETF's two Kalman filters, original state classifier and original 24-policy grid. Next, fit the optional simple thesis rule on the last 756 training return sessions using the fixed base policy and original detector. Require at least three completed trades, higher Sharpe, CAGR within 1 percentage point and drawdown within 2 points of the training control. Choose highest Sharpe, then CAGR, smaller probability floor, and longer persistence; otherwise disable it. Finally fit the symmetric fast HMM on all training fast slopes and replace the old fast detector for every ETF. Do not enable the separate online entry-value model.

This inheritance is a limitation: original policy thresholds and thesis settings were not jointly optimized under the final symmetric HMM. Their training used full-training model fits, so it is fitted calibration rather than a fully nested walk-forward backtest. A future refit must reproduce this declared sequence or declare a new algorithm version. “Family=slope” in old metadata identifies the source of the thresholds, not the detector running in the chosen version.

Limitations that matter for the portfolio test


    

  1. We have not demonstrated a general single-ETF alpha. The chosen later median IC5 is −.035; earlier +.034. Daily strategy/asset correlation medians are .655/.573. Positive net returns and lower exposure alone cannot distinguish timing skill from retained market exposure. More diversification may improve the portfolio but does not retroactively validate the signal.
    2. 

  2. Repeated inspection and selection bias. These eras have guided many hypotheses. Neither is a pristine holdout, and choosing this baseline now uses accumulated development evidence. Future rolling rankings must not use hindsight-selected ETFs or in-sample strategy NAV as if it had been observable trading performance.
    3. 

  3. Conditional model uncertainty is incomplete. Kalman slope variance assumes the chosen model and fitted parameters. HMM states classify serially correlated estimated slopes; they are not probabilities of profit or proof of a true economic regime. Slow and fast filters share the same observations. The imposed smoothness ranges do not prove a learned multiyear timescale.
    4. 

  4. Fixed parameters can become stale. Training volatility, noise, transition probabilities and thresholds do not refit in this baseline. Continuously updating states is not continuously learning new model parameters. A three-year history was not the universal fitting window. Range states can hide a shallow decline, while quick rebounds can produce false upward entries.
    5. 

  5. Closing protection is slow during shocks. The boundary is tested at the close and filled at a later open. Intraday falls, overnight gaps and execution delay can exceed the nominal initial distance by a large amount. It is not a maximum-loss guarantee. The frozen recipe has no independent portfolio disaster-loss budget.
    6. 

  6. Exit and re-entry are coupled. Rearming needs fast-up below .50 before a new entry. An exit can strand the strategy in cash during a continuing rally. The rule can also sell after a pullback and buy after a recovery; tighter exits alone do not solve this. No reviewer or automatic buyback is included.
    7. 

  7. Risk estimates can be misleading after inactivity. Cash days reduce measured strategy variance. A recent few-day win can inflate short Sharpe, while missing a rally creates a long low-risk record. The floor and covariance shrinkage are safeguards, not evidence of stable future risk. Loss correlations can rise when many sleeves trigger together.
    8. 

  8. Many ETFs need not mean many independent bets. A top-20 list can contain overlapping equity regions/sectors. The rank formula partly counts the same return information four times because return and Sharpe overlap. Report selected capital, actually invested breadth, effective N, correlations and exposure clusters. Larger N can dilute ranking edge as well as reduce concentration.
    9. 

  9. Counterfactual strategy and actual portfolio trades differ. A newly selected active ETF joins an ongoing virtual trade at a different price. Its existing stop refers to the virtual entry, not the portfolio's new cost basis. A selected sleeve may be removed before its strategy would exit. These differences require a real cash/share portfolio ledger and separate attribution for signals versus allocation changes.
    10. 

  10. Data and execution limits. Prices are from the pinned audited published batch; auditing does not prove historical publication vintages or eliminate survivorship bias in the supported pool. Missing data may not be filled or spliced. Returns are USD research, not an audited CNH result. Fractional adjusted units, zero cash yield and fixed fees omit spreads, capacity, taxes and actual order execution. Native accounting checks validate frozen fills and NAV, not economic predictability.
    11. 




Next experiment: monthly membership, daily strategy trading



Primary interpretation of your request: keep every supported ETF's virtual strategy running continuously, whether selected or not. On the last observed trading close of each month, build the rank table and choose top N. Set equal-capital or covariance-aware equal-risk sleeve budgets. Execute membership and capital changes at the next available open. Between selection dates, members follow their daily strategy signals. Cash stays with the selling sleeve. No daily top-up of the remaining active ETFs, no resetting the strategy on selection, and no unrequested new ETF-specific rules.


Entry when membership changes: if a newly selected virtual sleeve is already invested, acquire its exposure at the rebalance open using the last known signal state and inherited virtual protection. If it is flat, allocate cash and await its next buy. If a signal exit is due at that open, cash wins and avoid an unnecessary buy/sell round trip. Departing members are liquidated even if their strategy remains long; their virtual records continue. Existing selected sleeves retain state across capital changes. Report these joining/departing trades separately.


Two main allocations and one control: ranked equal capital; ranked equal strategy-risk contribution; unranked full-pool equal-capital strategy sleeves. N=6/10/15/20/30 is a sensitivity curve, not twenty named trading variants. Monthly membership is the main clock. A separately reported monthly-or-10%-target-change clock tests dynamic selection under the repository's research rules. Every version retains daily strategy exits and buys.


Choosing N: select only within chronological training/validation, prefer the largest near-optimal plateau under the formula above, and freeze before the next block. Also show fixed N curves so instability is visible. Use net Sharpe with return/drawdown guards; no absolute annual cost ceiling. An isolated best N on a reused evaluation is not an optimum. Test 63/252 as primary ranking windows and 21/126 as a declared sensitivity, with no weight optimization over the four rank components.


Avoid the warmup leak: the final 2019/2022 fitted models cannot generate supposedly out-of-sample pre-cutoff ranking histories. For the first frozen-fit portfolio replay, use the first 252 genuinely post-fit return observations as ranking warmup, and start the common portfolio evaluation only when every comparison has the required history. This places the start around 2021/2024; the exact common trading date must be recorded. Choosing N for these already-inspected eras remains development selection. A stronger later study must generate pre-decision rolling or cross-fitted strategy records, select N within inner time blocks, and evaluate untouched following blocks. Do not silently backfill ranks with full-training NAV.


Accounting: calculate ranking signals from net independent shadow-account returns, but calculate portfolio NAV from actual holdings and cash. Pay costs once on actual netted portfolio fills, including membership changes and daily signal trades. Do not compound net shadow returns and then charge the same signal costs again. Capital budgets drift between selection dates. Store target versus held weights separately. Risk uses strategy returns; portfolio drawdown still comes from the actual portfolio ledger.


Validation: full supported candidate pool, causal availability and common calendars; compare unranked strategy sleeves, a matched raw-ETF momentum selector, ETF buy-and-hold/cash, and FR25 only over compatible dates/data/currency. FR25 is an empirical incumbent, not a gold standard. Test 5/10/20 bp each side and one extra execution session, annual and regime results, turnover/cost attribution, selection stability, worst contributor and the weakest single-ETF chart each period. Judge incremental net portfolio performance before any promotion. No portfolio result is claimed in this report.
