One forecast for buying, holding and exiting

28 October 2026 · Full ETF pool · Three main comparisons

Research on repeatedly inspected development periods, through 16 October 2026. Reviewer selection uses training data only, but this is not a fresh prospective test. Daily OHLC stop executions are modeled. No overlay, promotion or live changes.

  • A — standing protection: unchanged reference strategy, original trend exits and gap-aware stops.
  • B — coherent price review: one per-ETF return forecast, based on price features, shared by holding and recovery. Reversal requires changed evidence.
  • C — coherent price/volume review: the same rule with three additional volume features. Each model abstains if chronological training does not support predictive improvement.

Do the trained forecasts improve?
__FORECASTS__

MSE is the squared error of the normalized next-session return forecast, median across risky ETFs. Lower is better. The constant benchmark predicts the final training mean. Forecast IC is Spearman correlation with the same target, excluding undefined constant forecasts. Evaluation uses every matured origin, including while cash; it is independent of the strategy's selected decision contexts. No online model influences trades in this round.


Mathematical definition
__EQUATIONS__

O/H/L/C use audited dividend/split-adjusted prices and V audited raw share volume. ATR is Wilder's 20-session true-range average, with a price-proportional floor. A flat bar has close location 0.5. EMA uses coefficient 2/(span+1). All features are available at the completed close. The intercept is neither clipped away nor standardized; I* penalizes the other coefficients, with only a 10−8 numerical penalty on the intercept.


One forecast with one target. The same feature vector and coefficients apply whether the account is invested or in cash. The target is the return from the next open to the following open, known only at that second open. This avoids the previous assumption that a recovery continues for ten sessions without future review exits. It is a forecast of the underlying asset, not a full dynamic-programming action value or the actual return of a stop-protected trade. Protection can close the position before the forecast endpoint. The one-session horizon is an engineering choice for this consistency test, not a claimed optimal trend timescale.


Each ETF is fitted separately. Use at most 756 matured origins after 252 warmup sessions. Three chronological validation blocks have width min(126, floor(sample size/5)), at least 50. Training expands before each block; every training label must mature strictly before the validation block begins. The scaler and coefficients are fitted anew inside each fold. Select the lowest mean validation MSE among an intercept and ridge penalties 1, 10 and 100, preferring a constant then stronger shrinkage in a tie. Review activates only for a nonconstant choice with more than 0.5% aggregate error improvement and improvement in at least two of three blocks. Otherwise the fitted forecast is shown but has no trading authority.


The final selected model is refitted on all mature training observations. No strategy-return search or HMM state enters reviewer calibration. Thus the reviewer folds are independent of the parent fit; this does not retrospectively make the parent HMM's original selection fully nested. Both evaluation eras have been inspected repeatedly and are development evidence. There is no final-refit re-selection using evaluation results.


Reversals need new information. A holding review can sell only below −0.05 ATR-return units and after a fall of more than 0.10 units from the entry-signal forecast. A recovery can buy only above +0.05 after round-trip fees and after an improvement of more than 0.10 from the completed sale-day forecast. These margins are fixed engineering choices, not estimated probabilities or evaluation-tuned parameters. A constant forecast cannot cause either evidence change. A recovery signal cannot simultaneously meet the learned exit condition on the same information.


Ordinary HMM entries retain priority and use their signal-date forecast as the anchor; a negative forecast alone does not veto them. Sale anchors use the completed sale-day close and are first consulted on a later day. Recovery is considered for 1–20 sessions after sale. The next executable open must remain within one signal-date ATR of signal close or the recovery order is cancelled. A later actual change in evidence may still justify a rapid exit; the rule does not ban all short trades.


HMM handoff and protection. Recovery temporarily defers HMM exits until upward-entry eligibility is confirmed, including during a delayed recovery order. Shared review and standing protection remain active. Original entries keep original exits. d is the inherited wide multiplier in aligned upward context, otherwise the tight multiplier; sigma is the inherited training risk unit. Initial risk scales with entry price, adopted stop levels never widen, and Peak is the running maximum of entry fill and subsequent closes. f is the per-side fee and s the additional stop slippage. No initial stop distance or position size is optimized in this round.


Execution, data and verification limits


Signals use completed closes and fill at the next open, or one further session in delay stress. A standing stop uses the level known before that day's low: if touched, fill at min(open, stop) less modeled slippage. Opening scheduled exits precede the stop test; protective fills cancel stale pending exits. Entry-day stops are allowed. Daily bars cannot establish spread, liquidity or the path through intraday jumps. No same-close fill is claimed.

Auditing does not prove historical publication vintages. Positions use fractional shares, fees on each side and terminal marking without forced sale. Raw HMM IC is unchanged across the three arms; forecast IC concerns the separate next-session return target. Cash earns zero. Delay stress uses unchanged forecasts at later execution, so it is deliberately sensitive to forecast decay.
