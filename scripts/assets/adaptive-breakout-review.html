What changed—and what the comparison can establish

Exit protection

Existing exits; an entry-price floor after a 1 ATR peak gain; a 0.5, 1 or 2 ATR trail activated after a 1 ATR gain; or the first lower close after that gain. A level is a closing decision threshold. Gaps can make the next-open fill worse.

Trend direction

Each ETF is classified as rising, falling or mixed from its price versus its 200-session average and that average's 63-session slope. Entry filters allow everything, avoid falling trends, or require rising trends. Existing positions still follow their exits. This is the ETF's own trend, not a global equity-market filter.

Range redesign

The existing horizontal box competes with a flat, volatility-scaled consolidation and a rising regression channel with contained residual fluctuations. Both use only prior closes and require volatility contraction. Short lookbacks: 21/63 sessions. Long lookbacks: 126/252. Original buffers, confirmation, initial risk, cooldown and continuation rules remain fitted per ETF.

Selection discipline

90 declared combinations per available ETF/domain/cutoff. Exit-only, trend-only, range-only and joint fits each compete against the unchanged control. Training selection requires sufficient trades, better worst-friction utility, and return/drawdown protection. The forced first-lower-close rule is reported separately.

All 52 ETFs are evaluated; BIL is excluded from risky-ETF summaries. Four early fits lack enough training history. Other sparse training evidence stays flagged. Zero interest on idle cash; 5/10/20 bp per side; next open or one additional session. No live or monitored strategy was changed.

Evidence and limitations

Alpha controls for the same ETF's return and the BIL cash benchmark. IC is the rank correlation of the desired long state with the next-open-to-21-session ETF return above BIL. Positive returns, or a better drawdown, do not establish predictive alpha. Repeatedly studied history and sparse trades limit inference. New flat/rising definitions are only the bounded candidates described here; a failure does not exhaust all definitions of consolidation.
