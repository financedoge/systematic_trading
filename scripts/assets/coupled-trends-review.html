Can a short upswing earn money inside a long downtrend?

TLT case inspection, followed by the same test across all 52 supported ETFs. Slow and fast latent trends interact through learned transition probabilities. No fixed trend lookback enters the new signals.

Previous report, now with exact date selection · Full ETF table · Frozen protocol · Exact TLT diagnostics

What happened in TLT

Late 2023: late recognition. The old selected 63-session detector first confirms up on December 14 and buys on December 15. The adjusted close rises 22.63% from October 19 to December 27, but the trade exits March 4, 2024 at a 4.55% net loss.

Mid-2024: late exit. The old model buys June 27. Its unrealized gain reaches 9.41% at the September 16 close; the October 30 exit keeps 0.47% after costs. This selected model had no protective stop.

The extrema describe the opportunity with hindsight; they were not supplied to the optimizer. New parameters were fitted separately for every ETF using history ending before each evaluation period. The two event intervals were declared before evaluating new models.

A probabilistic model may remain uncertain even where a long chart looks directional. The slow factor measures a latent contribution to expected daily return; it is not automatically the multi-year corridor seen by a human.

What this test supports—and what it does not

The new selected strategy captures the November–December 2023 rally: November 3 entry, January 5 exit, +9.24% net. For June–October 2024 it loses 0.98%: June 5–July 2 loses 2.28%, then August 2–September 26 earns 1.33%. It again exits near a local low in July and misses much of the recovery before August.

TLT's full later-period return is −17.81%, Sharpe −0.596, drawdown 25.13%; the old selected strategy was −18.53%, −0.656 and 20.36%. The independent model does better at −15.49%. Coupling is therefore not the demonstrated source of the rally capture.

The slow trend is still not identified reliably. Neither slow-up nor slow-down probability reaches 55% in any of TLT's 951 evaluation sessions. The coupled model's slow-down probability stays between 23.58% and 37.75%, and its total expected return stays positive. Consequently uniform and context exits produce identical TLT trades. Adding volume changes also leaves these selected TLT trades unchanged. The new architecture cannot yet support the intended distinction between a long decline and a brief rally.

Across the pool, selected median Sharpe is 0.316 later / 0.215 earlier, versus 0.319 / 0.214 previously. GLD return drops from 85.78% to 18.02%; SOXX from 152.05% to 117.27%. CORN, EWA and MCHI improve. No component acceptance gate or portfolio gate passes. The dynamic overlay earns 11.35% CAGR / 1.401 Sharpe versus FR25's 16.51% / 1.450, although drawdown improves from 8.65% to 6.15%.

Next research hypothesis: represent the slow component as a continuous latent price slope with uncertainty, so flattening can be measured directly; retain a faster switching component for rebounds. Learn adaptation/persistence from training rather than impose a fixed trend window. Test corridor recognition and recovery timing as well as returns. This is a proposed extension, not a fitted result, and needs another declared experiment.

Model and decision flow

Daily observations → nine joint states → forward probabilities → entry and context-dependent exit → next-open fill. Each factor has down/range/up states. Return means add a slow and a fast contribution; volume versions add daily log-volume changes. Gaussian emission variance is shared across states so state labels cannot simply become volatility buckets. Slow/fast separation still needs explicit persistence constraints: slow conditional stay probability 0.98–0.999; fast 0.65–0.97. These constrain durations, not how many past observations are read.

The coupled model estimates P(slow today | slow yesterday, fast yesterday) and P(fast today | fast yesterday, slow today). Its independent control removes these cross-factor dependencies. All probabilities used in trading are forward filtered; training-only smoothing is never used to relabel evaluation history. Two daily factors are a tractable instance of the broader factorial hidden-state approach; this experiment adds conditional transition coupling.

Buy on sufficient fast-up probability and positive expected next-session return, including during slow down. Uniform exits use a fast-down threshold and a trailing distance of 2/3/4 fitted daily return standard deviations. Context exits require stronger down evidence and double that distance when slow-up probability exceeds 55%. An adopted stop can tighten but never widen. After exit, fast-up probability must fall below 35% before a new entry is armed.

18 paired policy choices × independent/coupled × uniform/context × price/volume = 144 candidates per ETF/cutoff. Top two per family face all six cost/delay settings. Model drift and transition parameters are fitted by training likelihood with persistence priors; trading policy is selected by training net outcomes. This is in-sample calibration, not rolling model-refit validation.

Signals below 50% are allowed by the declared policy grid because the three states can remain ambiguous. These probabilities are model outputs, not calibrated trading confidence. Protection is tested at the completed close and filled at the next open, or one extra session later. It is not an intraday order or a guaranteed loss cap. Distance uses training volatility; volatility-spike tightening is deliberately excluded from this comparison.
