What was fitted: each ETF already has independently fitted entry/risk parameters. Three distinct failure cases

SLV long: worst net return

−20.31%, versus +84.41% previously and +133.92% buy and hold. Five of six trades lose. The initial risk floor is 18.5–24.0% below entry, while formation of a positive local slope can be late. In the October 2024 trade, the first support only forms December 10, well below the entry price; it protects a small recovery after a decline, not an established profitable advance.

The March 2025 trade also shows execution risk: the decision close is 28.89, below the 30.05 boundary, and the next open is 27.96. A closing boundary cannot guarantee a sale at that boundary. The trade loses 8.96%.

After June 25, 2025, no new position is taken through the cutoff. SLV rises 58.35% from that sale price. Continuation re-entry is off; cooldown is only five sessions, so cooldown alone does not explain the long absence.

TLT short: repeated entries that fail to trend

−15.93%, versus −12.73% previously. Inspect the ledger for initial-risk and time exits: an improved profit exit has little to protect when the entry does not develop into a rising trend. This is a different problem from SLV's missed continuation.

SOXX short: largest deterioration, still profitable

+24.62%, down from +187.66% with the previous exit. A positive outcome can still destroy much of the earlier trend capture. SOXX has continuation enabled, but retains a 20-session cooldown and its fitted continuation conditions. Turning re-entry on alone is not sufficient evidence of a solution.

These cases were selected after seeing the results for diagnosis. They must not become an ETF exclusion list or a basis for choosing new parameters on this sample.

What this diagnosis suggests testing next

The implementation matches the declared mathematics and timing. The remaining weaknesses are in the rule design: positive local slope does not establish a durable post-breakout uptrend; large initial risk can allow a failed entry to deteriorate before support appears; and an exit/re-entry pair can repeatedly sell a continuing trend without getting back in.

A next experiment should separate initial-breakout failure from profit protection, test causal higher-low or multiple-timescale support, and coordinate continuation re-entry with the exit. Keep existing strong cases as return/drawdown controls, fit on training only, and retain the full pool, cost/delay sensitivities and dynamic/monthly portfolio comparisons. No redesign has been fitted or declared successful here.

Verification and limits

Same pinned audited published adjusted OHLC and audited raw volume through October 16, 2026. The current supported pool has historical membership and publication-vintage limitations. Both periods have been inspected repeatedly; this is development evidence, not a fresh holdout. Alpha is annualized regression intercept above BIL after controlling for the same ETF's excess return; Sharpe uses zero risk-free return. Calmar uses actual maximum drawdown. Existing dynamic/monthly portfolio controls remain in the linked study; those selected portfolios retained baseline exits in the later period and do not validate the fixed trend rule as a SOTA overlay.
