# Research guidance for concentration and repeatability

Apply these checks when comparing portfolio recipes. They follow the
[cohort discussion](../research/expanded-fr25-cohort-discussion-20261103.json),
[breadth experiment](../research/fr25-breadth-20261103-v1/index.html) and
[winner-withholding experiment](../research/etf-stability-20261103-v1/index.html).

1. **Choose before evaluation.** Freeze the family, objective, friction
   settings, guardrails and tie-break. Select on training data and seal the
   choice before evaluating it. Record prior use of both windows and the
   number of trials. Reserve claims of fresh validation for observations
   collected after a freeze.
2. **Compare like periods and meaningful returns.** Show net CAGR and net
   Sharpe in chronological training subdivisions and evaluation, alongside
   cumulative return, drawdown, risk exposure, BIL, cash, turnover and fees.
   Report the gaps and their uncertainty. A stability objective needs a
   declared return or risk budget so moving to cash cannot win by default.
   The 5.57% floor belonged to one experiment. Set and justify the comparator
   and any floor separately for each study.
3. **Measure dependence on winners.** For the selected recipe and incumbent,
   report top contributors in each era, net P&L as a share of opening NAV,
   and shares of net gains and of positive contributions. Show signed P&L
   when a net-share denominator is small or negative. As a stress, move the
   top one and three risky ETF weights to cash, preserve other target weights
   and adoption dates, and recalculate net results. Also carry training leaders
   into evaluation. Label retrospective leader exclusions as sensitivity tests.
   A fund-level or theme exclusion alone cannot establish economic causation.
4. **Explain changes in portfolio risk.** When the universe changes, distinguish
   correlations in the pool from those among held ETFs. Inspect risk exposure,
   individual holding volatility and defensive dates. State the denominator
   and rounding of breadth rules. Hold width fixed to isolate breadth and
   breadth fixed to isolate width. Count the decisions where each rule binds.
5. **Keep the trade-off visible.** Report performance floors, stress costs,
   execution delays, named-asset dependence and nearby settings. Smaller
   historical gaps can coexist with low growth, large drawdowns or wide
   uncertainty. Retain counterexamples and test new hypotheses in a separate
   declared comparison. Alpha54, FR25 and any specific breadth or return
   threshold remain empirical references.
